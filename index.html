Self-Aligned Tetrahedral Blade Architecture
for Hybrid Lift–Drag Vertical-Axis Wind Turbines

A geometrically constrained tetrahedral wing derived entirely from the golden ratio — no experimental data, a single design parameter.

Abstract

We propose extending each planar golden-gnomon (108°–36°–36°) wing of a regular-icosahedron-based VAWT into a self-aligned tetrahedron T(P, V0, V1, V2). The apex P is defined at distance L/4 from V0 along the base symmetry axis at elevation angle θ = arctan(1/φ) = 31.7175°, where φ = (1+√5)/2 is the golden ratio. This angle is proven to equal half the supplement of the regular dodecahedron dihedral angle (Theorem 1). The construction yields a double quarter-point property (Theorem 2): simultaneously |V0P| = L/4 and P lies at s = 1/4 of the unshared icosahedron ring edge. All wing dimensions reduce to integer multiples of L/4, enabling direct fabrication from a single reference length.

Six placement axioms uniquely determine the ten-wing arrangement up to icosahedron symmetry (Theorem 3). The two candidate arrangements are physically identical — related by the antipodal map N↔S — analogous to polarity reversal in electromagnetic motors (Corollary 1). We hypothesize a three-fold enhancement: aerodynamic lift-like differential ΔC_L, 90°-phase-separated from the drag differential ΔC_D, and increased structural redundancy through 10 additional load paths.

1. Geometric Construction

1.1 The golden elevation angle

Starting from a planar golden-gnomon wing, the apex P is placed above the base plane at elevation angle θ derived directly from the golden ratio:

Theorem 1 — Dodecahedral origin of θ

The following are equivalent:

θ = arctan(1/φ) = arctan(2)/2 ≈ 31.7175°

Proof. The regular dodecahedron dihedral angle is α = π − arctan(2) ≈ 116.565°. Its supplement is arctan(2) ≈ 63.435°. Applying the half-angle tangent formula: tan(arctan(2)/2) = (2/√5)/(1+1/√5) = 2/(√5+1) = (√5−1)/2 = 1/φ. ∎

Since the golden-gnomon triangle (108°–36°–36°) is the face of the regular dodecahedron, this elevation angle is intrinsic to the golden-ratio geometric family — not an externally imposed parameter.

1.2 Definition of the apex P

Definition 1 — Apex P

Let L = |V0V1| = |V0V2|, M = (V1+V2)/2, and n̂ the outward unit normal to the base plane. The apex P is defined as:

P = V0 + (L/4) · [ cosθ · ûV0M + sinθ · n̂ ]

where θ = arctan(1/φ). Thus |V0P| = L/4 exactly.

1.3 The double quarter-point property

Theorem 2 — Double quarter-point property

For T_N1 (V0=U₁, V1=N, V2=L₁), the apex P defined by Definition 1 satisfies simultaneously:

  1. |V0P| = L/4 (one quarter of the reference edge length)
  2. P lies at s = 1/4 of icosahedron edge U₁–U₅

Both hold for all ten wings by 5-fold rotational and antipodal symmetry.

Remark — Manufacturability

The double quarter-point property reduces all wing dimensions to integer multiples of L/4. Given one reference length L, every dimension follows: mark the quarter-point (s = 1/4) of each ring edge — that is P. No auxiliary angle calculation is required during fabrication.

2. Unique Placement Arrangement

2.1 Six placement axioms

  • ①Edge anchoring: V0–V1 and V0–V2 are icosahedron edges.
  • ②Long base free: V1–V2 is not an icosahedron edge (aerodynamically active long base φL).
  • ③Global edge non-sharing: No two wings among the ten share any edge.
  • ④V0–V2 pair uniqueness: No two wings share the same V0–V2 vertex pair.
  • ⑤P on ring edge: Each P lies on a U-ring edge (N-pole) or L-ring edge (S-pole) at s = 1/4.
  • ⑥Non-intersection: No two wings intersect in 3D space.

Theorem 3 — Unique admissible arrangement

Subject to Axioms 1–6, there exists exactly one admissible arrangement up to icosahedron symmetry. Its canonical form (Pattern A):

N-pole wings T_N(i): V0=Ui, V1=N, V2=Li — P on edge Ui–Ui+1 at s=1/4

S-pole wings T_S(i): V0=Li, V1=S, V2=Ui+1 — P on edge Li–Li+1 at s=1/4 (indices mod 5)

2.2 Chiral equivalence — electromagnetic pole analogy

Corollary 1 — Chiral equivalence

The two candidate arrangements are physically identical. The antipodal map v → −v (N↔S exchange) converts one to the other via index relabeling (i → i+2 mod 5), with no geometric change. This mirrors the electromagnetic convention: reversing the N/S labels on a magnet reverses the motor's rotation sense without changing the physical device. The VAWT rotor is a single structure; its CW or CCW bias depends only on which pole is called N.

3. Aerodynamic Phase Model (Hypothesis)

3.1 Drag differential — planar baseline

In the planar configuration, drag varies with rotation phase θ as:

C_D(θ) = C̄_D + (ΔC_D / 2) · cos θ    (1)

3.2 Hypothesized lift-like differential

The off-center apex P introduces an orientation-dependent lift-like differential hypothesized to follow:

C_L(θ) = (ΔC_L / 2) · sin θ    (2)

Drag peaks at θ = 0° (cup mode); lift peaks at θ = 90° (V0-leading). The 90° phase separation decouples the two torque sources in time.

3.3 Combined cycle-averaged torque

⟨T⟩ ∝ R · Aproj · ½ρVrel² · (ΔC_D + ΔC_L)    (3)

The ten wings at 36° azimuthal spacing produce T(θ + 36°) = T(θ): Fourier harmonics only at multiples of 10, predicting low torque ripple.
